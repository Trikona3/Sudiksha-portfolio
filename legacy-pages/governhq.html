GovernHQ

Case Study

Project Snapshot

Governance layer for autonomous AI agents in enterprise systems

GovernHQ is an enterprise AI agent governance MVP that intercepts agent actions before execution, evaluates them against policy, and gives operations teams a live control layer for approvals, blocks, audits, and monitoring.

ORG PM Accelerator ROLE Co-UX Designer · Research · IA · Prototype SCOPE Enterprise AI Governance MVP

Project Description

I worked as a co-UX designer on a two-person design team. We defined the product structure, information architecture, and end-to-end prototype for a platform used by enterprise AI admins, security teams, and operations stakeholders. The product was designed to evaluate agent actions in real time, surface risk clearly, and make policy enforcement reviewable instead of invisible.

What I Worked On

Product structure

Helped define the core platform architecture across overview, ledger, agents, shield, policies, settings, and onboarding.

User research

Framed the needs of enterprise AI admins, governance teams, and operators who need confidence before autonomous actions go live.

Flow prototyping

Prototyped the full product flow, from onboarding and policy setup to live monitoring, audit review, and agent management.

Design Decisions

Made the decision log the center of the experience

The most important behavior to review is what an agent tried to do and whether it was allowed, blocked, or paused, so the decision log became the main focus instead of a secondary table.

Used a sidebar for system-level context

Stats, system health, recent activity, and pending approvals were moved into a supporting sidebar so the main pane could stay focused on operational review.

Separated governance into distinct mental models

Agents, Shield, Policies, and Ledger each answer a different governance question, so the IA was structured around those tasks rather than mixing everything into one dashboard.

Made onboarding progressive instead of overwhelming

The setup flow breaks adoption into connected steps: connect data, configure agents, define access rules, then launch and monitor.

User Personas

AI Operations Lead

Owns live agent performance and approvals

Needs to quickly understand which agent actions are safe, which need review, and where operational bottlenecks are forming.

Security & Compliance Manager

Defines policy and reduces risk exposure

Needs strong audit trails, explainable blocks, enforcement controls, and confidence that sensitive actions cannot slip through.

Platform Administrator

Configures agents, access, and integrations

Needs a setup flow that makes complex governance infrastructure feel manageable, structured, and operationally clear.

USP

Real-time interception

GovernHQ acts before agent actions execute, which makes governance proactive instead of post-incident.

Policy + audit in one system

Teams can define rules, inspect outcomes, and trace every action in one connected workflow rather than across disconnected tools.

Human review where it matters

The product creates a clear decision layer for paused or high-risk actions instead of forcing all-or-nothing autonomy.

User Flow

1. Connect

Add data sources and systems

2. Configure

Set up agents, roles, and capabilities

3. Govern

Define policies, thresholds, and approval rules

4. Monitor

Review decisions, logs, health, and blocked actions

User Journey Map

Setup phase

The admin connects data sources, configures agents, and establishes access rules, moving from uncertainty to a structured operational baseline.

Live governance phase

Operations and governance teams monitor decision logs, pending approvals, and anomaly signals to decide when to allow, pause, or block agent behavior.

Policy refinement phase

Teams learn from blocked and paused behavior, then refine policies and thresholds to improve both safety and throughput over time.

Challenges & Learnings

Challenge: making invisible AI risk legible

Governance products can easily become too abstract. We had to translate invisible agent decisions into a system that felt concrete, reviewable, and urgent enough to act on.

Challenge: balancing monitoring with control

The product needed to support both passive visibility and direct intervention, without overwhelming users with too many governance controls at once.

Learning: hierarchy matters more in enterprise tools

Putting the decision log at the center and moving summary metrics to the sidebar made the product feel much clearer because the most actionable information stayed dominant.

Learning: onboarding is part of trust-building

Breaking setup into guided steps helped make a technically complex product feel safer and more adoptable for enterprise teams.

Onboarding Flow

The onboarding experience was designed as a guided progression so teams could move from setup to operational readiness without having to understand the full platform all at once.

Settings and Platform Administration

The settings area extends the governance model into organization setup, API credentials, integrations, and billing so the platform feels operationally complete.

Key Screens

Overview

A live command center showing decision history, pending approvals, recent activity, and system health at a glance.

Agents and action breakdown

An agent management layer where teams can inspect activity, capabilities, and per-agent action patterns.

Ledger and auditability

A tamper-proof record of every action with searchable entries, timestamps, and expandable reasoning for blocked actions.

Shield and policy controls

Configurable protection controls let teams tune enforcement strictness, thresholds, and emergency behavior without leaving the product.

Policy management

Policies were structured to be easy to scan, edit, duplicate, and scope to specific agents.

Outcome

GovernHQ turned a complex governance problem into a navigable product system: teams can review autonomous actions, understand why something was blocked, configure enforcement rules, and launch agents with more confidence and control.
